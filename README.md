# BIBO-3D-Printer
 Official and unofficial support files for the Makerbot Replicator open source clone dual extruder BIBO 3D Printer. BIBO printer is an open source clone of the makerbot replicator 1 or 2 family. (Flashforge, CTC, etc are also in this clone family)
There are some things which are direct matches and other things which are custom to the BIBO.

The manufacturer has gone out of business. This information is retained to help users

-- Q: How can I know the print failure cause is from the printer calibration or slicing settings?

-- A: Please kindly print the test gcode files with the stock PLA filament: https://drive.google.com/file/d/1y2nqfK7Dx2IxXu9M2p_PNU8A4FyewKW7/view?usp=sharing

  If the print is ok, usually the cause is slicing settings. Please follow the instructions to import our slicing settings again.

-- Q: My print is not sticking to the bed. How do I solve this problem?

-- A: First layer adhesion can be challenging for new users. Getting your bed leveling correct is the first step. Please apply more glue or hairspray on the glass bed and wait for it to dry first. You can also loosen the three bed adjustment wing nuts under the bed a fraction of a turn to lift the bed a little to have better print adhesion.

-- Q: How Do I clean my clogged hot end?

-- A: You can push down the filament or metal nozzle cleaning needles by hand again and again to the heated hot end to clean a clogged hot end, and this way usually works. The metal nozzle cleaning needles under the diameter 0.4mm are ok. For example, this loop metal stick can help from the top to push a clog out: www.amazon.com/dp/B01COSU6CW/

-- Q: What do you recommend to replace the parts on BIBO 3d printer?

-- A: There is a community maintained parts document. The printer is just an open source clone, the challenge is identifying the correct part and specification to replace the part. This is why the communiuty maintained parts document helps. 

Disable WiFi function (click Settings-WiFi-Disable on the touch screen), the WIFI is not recommended.
 
-- Q: When I try to print large or tall models, the other nozzle not printing will knock the print. How to solve this problem?

-- A: This problem is always caused by the nozzles are too close to the bed or the print is warping. Usually we can tighten three wing nuts under the bed a little or heat the other nozzle at 165 degrees celsius, so the other hot nozzle will not ruin the print during printing.
there are jigs on thingiverse to aid in nozzle alignment
